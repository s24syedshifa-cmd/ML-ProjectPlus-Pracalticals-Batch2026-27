About Our Project

Handwritten digit recognition is the task of teaching a computer to read digits from 0 to 9 written by hand. It is a classic machine learning problem with real uses such as reading postal codes, bank cheques, forms and exam sheets. Our system lets you draw a digit or upload an image, then predicts the digit and shows how confident the model is.

How It Works

1

Input

Draw on the canvas or upload a PNG/JPG image.

2

Preprocessing

The image is converted to grayscale, cleaned, cropped, centred and resized to 28 × 28 pixels, the same format as the training data.

3

Prediction

The 784 pixel values are passed to the trained MLP model, which outputs a probability for each digit.

4

Result

The digit with the highest probability is shown along with its confidence percentage.

Dataset

The models were trained and tested on the MNIST dataset: 70,000 grayscale images of handwritten digits, each 28 × 28 pixels, split into 60,000 training and 10,000 testing images.

Algorithms Compared

Three algorithms, KNN, SVM and MLP, were evaluated on MNIST. The Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP), a simple neural network, gave the best accuracy and is the model used in this system.

Tips for Best Results

  • Write one digit at a time, large and clear.
  • Use a plain background and keep the digit in the centre.
  • Use a thick pen or marker for photos of handwriting.
  • Click Clear or Clear Image before trying the next digit.

Limitations

The system recognises single digits from 0 to 9 only. Very untidy writing, several digits in one image, or low-contrast photos can reduce accuracy.
